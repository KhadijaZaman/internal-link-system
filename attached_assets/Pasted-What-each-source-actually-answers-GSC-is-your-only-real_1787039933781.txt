What each source actually answers

GSC is your only real demand and SERP data. The five analyses worth building:

Query-to-page integrity. Find queries where more than one URL picks up impressions, and queries where the wrong URL ranks. Cannibalization and mismapping are the cheapest fixes you have.
CTR against the expected curve for that position, not raw CTR. Pages sitting below the curve have a title/snippet/SERP-feature problem, not a ranking problem. Pages above it are being under-served by rank.
Striking distance weighted by impressions, not by position. Position 11 on 400 impressions beats position 6 on 12.
Query discovery over time per URL. Count of distinct queries a page earns after an edit. This is how you measure whether coverage actually expanded, separate from whether rank moved.
Crawl stats and page indexing trend, segmented by template. Indexing decay by template is usually a structural problem, not a content one.

GA4 answers what the click was worth. Segment everything to organic first, then:

Engagement rate and conversion rate by landing page, compared against the page's GSC query intent. Informational pages converting at BOFU rates means your intent mapping is off.
Organic as assist versus organic as entry. Path exploration, not last-click. Otherwise you will kill pages that feed the funnel.
Internal site search terms. Demand you have failed to cover, straight from users.
Scroll and engagement time by template, before and after a redesign. This is the only honest read on whether a layout change helped.
Device split on the pages where GSC shows mobile position gaps.

Bing Webmaster Tools is cheap and mostly ignored. Worth the hour:

Query mix differs from Google, and often skews to a different buyer profile. Compare the two query sets for terms Google never surfaces to you.
Its crawl and index reporting is more explicit about why a URL was dropped than Google's is (recalled, confirm against your own property).
Free backlink data and referring-domain view.
IndexNow submission and its effect on time-to-index (recalled, and testable: submit a batch, log discovery dates).
Bing's index feeds Copilot and, reportedly, parts of other assistants' grounding. If AI visibility matters to you, Bing indexation is a prerequisite worth verifying rather than assuming (inferred, verify with a current source before you put it in a deck).
Five traps that break the analysis
GSC anonymizes low-volume queries, so query totals never sum to page totals. Never report a "share of queries" figure from that data.
Average position is an average across a skewed distribution. Use impression-weighted buckets instead.
GSC clicks and GA4 sessions do not reconcile. Different definitions, plus consent and blocker loss. Pick one as the source of truth per metric and say so.
Domain property versus URL-prefix property changes your numbers. Confirm which you're pulling before comparing periods.
Rankings flat but clicks down usually means a SERP feature took the click, not that you lost anything. Check the SERP before you touch the page.
The three joins that actually produce decisions

Query cluster → landing page → conversion value tells you where to invest.
High impressions plus low CTR plus decent conversion when clicked tells you to rewrite titles, not content.
High clicks plus low engagement plus zero conversion tells you the page matched a query it shouldn't rank for.
